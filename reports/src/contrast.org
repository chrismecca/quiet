* Purpose

This report records the canonical anchors and derived roles of quiet-day and
quiet-night, and the evaluation of every quiet-core contrast constraint
against them.

The tables are generated by =quiet report= from =spec/quiet-core.json=,
=spec/quiet-day.json=, and =spec/quiet-night.json=.  Constraints are
evaluated by the same code as =quiet check=, so this report and the command
cannot disagree.

The tables document what the reference implementation computes.  Independent
cross-checks of the underlying arithmetic live in the frozen test fixtures
under =internal/=.

* Method

Colors are sRGB with a D65 white point and standard dynamic range.

Contrast is the WCAG 2.x ratio of relative luminances, shown as /Y/.

Perceptual coordinates are OKLCH: lightness /L/, chroma /C/, and hue /h/ in
degrees.  A hue is shown only when chroma is at least 0.020; below that, a
numerically defined hue angle carries no meaning.

Ratios are printed to two decimals.  Pass or fail is decided on unrounded
values.

* Tightest margins

The constraints with the least headroom across both adaptations, printed to
three decimals so that a narrow pass does not read as a failure.

#+CAPTION: Lowest margins across both adaptations.
#+ATTR_LATEX: :font \small
#+INCLUDE: "../data/constraints-tightest.org"

quiet-day's chromatic and secondary foregrounds sit close to the 4.5:1
floor on Surface and Structure.  This is deliberate: quiet-core places
quiet-day's four chromatic foregrounds on a nearly common lightness and
contrast plane.

The consequence is a constraint on change control.  Any proposed change
that lowers quiet-day Surface lightness, or lightens Mineral, Moss, or
Muted, is likely to fail the contract unless it is compensated elsewhere.

quiet-night has more headroom.  Its tightest relationship is Rust on
Structure.
