#+TITLE: Color-vision deficiency screening
#+SUBTITLE: Chromatic families under simulated dichromacy
#+SETUPFILE: setup.org

* Purpose

quiet-core states that its chromatic families have been screened under
simulated protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia, and that semantic
redundancy, not hue distance, is the conformance mechanism.

This report reproduces that screening from the canonical palettes, for the
chromatic anchors and for the line washes.

* Method

Simulation uses the full-severity matrices of Machado, Oliveira, and
Fernandes (2009), applied in linear sRGB.  Simulated colors are analysis
values: they are neither clipped nor gamut-checked.

Distances are Euclidean in OKLab, written ΔE.  Both colors of a pair are
simulated, as a viewer with the deficiency sees both.

quiet-core assigns no threshold of distinguishability to ΔE, and this
report does not introduce one.  The tables are for comparison: between
pairs, and between normal and simulated vision.

As expected for red--green deficiencies, the Moss and Rust pair loses the
most distance under protanopia and deuteranopia.  Under tritanopia, Mineral
and Moss, and Ochre and Rust, draw closer while the other pairs separate.

In quiet-day, Ochre and Rust are the closest pair even with normal vision.
quiet-core already forbids distinguishing warning from error by Ochre
versus Rust alone.

* Line washes

#+CAPTION: Distance between line washes, quiet-night.
#+ATTR_LATEX: :font \small

Washes are faint by design, so their distances are small in every
condition.  The consequential case is a diff: additions use the Moss wash
and deletions the Rust wash.

Under deuteranopia those two washes nearly coincide in both adaptations.
A reader with deuteranopia cannot tell an added line from a deleted line by
its background.

This confirms the existing quiet-core rule rather than adding one: Wash
supports meaning but must not be its sole carrier.  Ports must keep the
host's =+= and =-= markers, gutter signs, or equivalent cues for diff and
diagnostic state.

* Foreground contrast under simulation

WCAG contrast is defined on the colors actually displayed, not on a
simulation.  The table is therefore informative: it shows how the luminance
a viewer perceives shifts under each deficiency.

#+ATTR_LATEX: :font \small
#+INCLUDE: "../data/cvd-contrast-day.org"

#+CAPTION: Chromatic anchors on Field, quiet-night.
#+ATTR_LATEX: :font \small
#+INCLUDE: "../data/cvd-contrast-night.org"

Because quiet-day's chromatic foregrounds sit close to 4.5:1, small
simulated luminance shifts take some of them below it: Mineral and Moss
under protanopia, and Rust under deuteranopia.  quiet-night keeps every anchor
above 4.5:1 under all three simulations.

These are not conformance failures.  They are evidence for any future
change-control proposal to raise quiet-day's chromatic contrast.

* Limitations

Simulation is a screening tool.  It does not replace evaluation with people
who have color-vision deficiencies.

Only full-severity dichromacy is simulated.  Anomalous trichromacy, which
is more common, produces smaller shifts than those shown here.
