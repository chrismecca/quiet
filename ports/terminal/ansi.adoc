= Quiet ANSI mapping
:toc:

The ANSI terminal model requires sixteen indexed colors.

Quiet Core defines ten canonical anchors and does not contain dedicated
magenta, cyan, or bright-color anchors.

The reference terminal port therefore maps existing roles where possible
and derives terminal-local extensions where required.

These extensions are part of the terminal reference port.

They are not Quiet Core anchors.

== Normal chromatic slots

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| ANSI | Quiet mapping

| 1 -- red
| Rust

| 2 -- green
| Moss

| 3 -- yellow
| Ochre

| 4 -- blue
| Mineral
|===

== Cyan

Quiet Core has no cyan anchor.

ANSI cyan is a terminal-local extension derived between Moss and Mineral in
OKLCH.

Its lightness and chroma are the arithmetic means of Moss and Mineral.

Its hue is the shortest-arc midpoint between those two hue families.

Current results:

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Mode | Cyan

| Quiet Day
| `#4C706B`

| Quiet Night
| `#7A9C93`
|===

== Magenta

Quiet Core has no magenta anchor.

ANSI magenta fills the otherwise unoccupied hue interval from Mineral,
through 360 degrees, to Rust.

Its lightness and chroma are the arithmetic means of Mineral and Rust.

Its hue is the midpoint of that outer interval.

The opposite interval is deliberately not used because it passes through
the existing Moss and Ochre families.

Current results:

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Mode | Magenta

| Quiet Day
| `#755E82`

| Quiet Night
| `#9C89B4`
|===

== Bright chromatic slots

ANSI bright chromatic colors are derived from their corresponding base
color in OKLCH.

The current candidate rule is:

----
bright.L = base.L + 0.010
bright.C = base.C * 1.20
bright.H = base.H
----

The rule gives `bright` the same interpretation in Quiet Day and Quiet
Night:

* modestly greater perceptual lightness;
* modestly greater chroma;
* unchanged hue family.

The previous experiment moved bright colors toward Ink in OKLab.

That rule was rejected because it made Quiet Day bright backgrounds darker
than their corresponding normal backgrounds.

Each bright chromatic color must:

* remain in sRGB gamut;
* retain at least `4.5:1` contrast against Field;
* have greater OKLCH lightness than its base color;
* have greater OKLCH chroma than its base color.

== Neutral slots

The four ANSI neutral slots use existing Quiet neutrals ordered by
perceptual lightness.

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| ANSI | Mapping

| 0 -- black
| darker of Field and Ink

| 8 -- bright black
| darker of Muted and Line

| 7 -- white
| lighter of Muted and Line

| 15 -- bright white
| lighter of Field and Ink
|===

This produces a monotonic neutral ramp in both Quiet Day and Quiet Night.

=== ANSI neutral limitation

ANSI neutral slots are overloaded as both foreground and background colors.

In Quiet Day, ANSI bright white approaches Field and may have little or no
foreground contrast on the default background.

In Quiet Night, ANSI black approaches Field and may have little or no
foreground contrast on the default background.

This behavior is intentional in the reference mapping.

The ANSI model cannot simultaneously preserve conventional black/white
endpoint semantics and guarantee readable foreground contrast for every
neutral slot in both light and dark adaptations.

Applications should not rely on ANSI 0 or ANSI 15 as universally readable
foreground colors.

== Semantic limitation

ANSI applications may assign their own meanings to indexed colors.

A terminal port therefore cannot guarantee Quiet Core semantics for
arbitrary third-party software.

The reference mapping preserves Quiet's color families and restrained
character while remaining compatible with the conventional ANSI model.
